## Who writes this

I'm **Justin Kindrix** — a software and network engineer who has spent most of a
career inside internet service providers, on the side of the work most customers
never see: the core, the automation, the monitoring, and the tooling that keeps a
network alive.

- At **CenturyLink (now Lumen)**, I helped stand up a **fixed wireless access**
  network built to reach rural customers under federal CAF-II grants — working
  the virtual core (EPC/MME, network management) of a 4G/5G buildout from the
  moment it was being switched on through the first FWA subscribers and the
  towers that followed. I came up through DSL and IPTV operations before that.
- At **SkyRider Communications / ArkLaNet**, a regional line-of-sight WISP
  running ~70 towers across the Mid-South, I built the Python systems that
  backed up and inventoried the network's radios and routers — MikroTik,
  Ubiquiti, Cambium, and more — across core, distribution, and access layers.
- Today I'm a Principal Engineer. I still build the kind of automation and
  tooling this handbook will lean on.

That's the honest shape of my experience: deep on the network, software, and
operations side of real ISPs — including a genuine WISP and a carrier fixed
wireless deployment — with lab experience on the radio hardware itself rather
than years on a tower. Where this handbook reaches past what I've done
hands-on, it says so, and the research behind it is shown, not asserted.

This guide is also a journey I'm walking in the open: I'm using it to help a
friend start their own WISP from scratch. They're the first reader and the
first test pilot. If it works for them, it earns the right to be here.

Find me on [GitHub](https://github.com/jkindrix) and
[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jkindrix).

## Scope and honesty

US-first. Every journey and reference page carries a last-reviewed date and a
depth badge (`overview` / `working` / `comprehensive`) so you always know
whether you're reading a summary or the full treatment. Pages that name specific equipment or
prices are flagged internally for shorter review cycles, because that
information goes stale fastest.

## Corrections

The source is [public on GitHub](https://github.com/jkindrix/wisphandbook).
Every page has an "Edit this page" link and a "Report outdated info" link that
pre-fills an issue. If you run a WISP and something here is wrong or out of
date, telling me is the most valuable thing you can do — a reference work is
maintained or it is dead.

## Disclaimer

This handbook describes the landscape; it is not professional training. Tower
climbing, electrical work, structural attachment, taxes, and law each have real
professionals and real certifications. Where a page touches one of those, it
says so and tells you when to hire one. Nothing here is legal, financial, or
safety advice.

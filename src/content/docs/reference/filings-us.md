The obligations below begin **at your first customer**, not at some comfortable
future size. This page is overview-depth: enough to know what exists and when
it's due. Verify current rules with the FCC before relying on it — deadlines
and requirements change.

## Broadband Data Collection (BDC)

The big one, and the one new operators most often learn about late:

- **Who must file:** every facilities-based provider of fixed or mobile
  broadband with **one or more end-user connections in service**. There is no
  small-provider exemption — a WISP with three customers files.
- **What:** your broadband availability data (where you offer service, at what
  speeds) into the FCC's BDC system, against the FCC's location fabric.
- **When:** twice a year. Data as of **December 31 is due March 1**; data as
  of **June 30 is due September 1**.
- **Start here:** [fcc.gov/BroadbandData](https://www.fcc.gov/BroadbandData)
  — you'll need an FCC Registration Number (FRN) and a filer account before
  your first window.

Filing accurately matters beyond compliance: BDC data feeds the national
broadband maps that determine where subsidy programs send competitors. Claiming
coverage you can't deliver invites challenges; under-claiming invites
subsidized overbuilders into your footprint.

## The rest of the landscape

At overview depth, be aware these exist and ask a telecom-savvy accountant or
attorney which apply to your specific situation:

- **FCC Registration Number (FRN)** — needed before any FCC filing; free and
  immediate.
- **Form 499 / USF** — primarily for providers of telecom services and
  interconnected VoIP; a broadband-only WISP may have minimal obligations
  here, but the moment you add voice service, this changes.
- **CALEA** — lawful-intercept capability requirements apply to facilities-
  based broadband providers; in practice, small operators address this with a
  written policy and capable equipment.
- **State and local** — business registration, sales-tax treatment of
  equipment and install fees (internet *access* itself is federally protected
  from state taxation in the US), pole-attachment and right-of-way rules if
  you expand to aerial plant.
